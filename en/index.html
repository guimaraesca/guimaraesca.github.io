MEC · UNESCO · World Bank · IDB · OEI

Before data and algorithms came the classroom. I started teaching in 2009 at CENEP-RN, in the state school network of Rio Grande do Norte, and spent more than a decade in teaching — coordinating tutors and mentor teachers at UFRN's Instituto Metrópole Digital (IMD), and lecturing at IFRN and UNIFACEX. That experience taught me how students and teachers actually relate to technology, and it remains the yardstick for everything I build. Then came data products for six state networks at Instituto Unibanco, the technical migration of a national school-management system at the Ministry of Education and, today, generative-AI engines for textbook production.

My method rests on a demanding premise: trustworthy AI depends on governed data, and governed data depends on knowing who decides, with what evidence, and under which safeguards. Every project therefore weaves three layers that rarely talk to each other. Engineering builds the pipelines, predictive models and dashboards that run the operation. Governance defines roles, quality, interoperability and data-protection compliance, so data is reliable at the source. And evaluation sets the indicators that show, without self-deception, whether the policy is producing its intended effect.

Three degrees support this path. Each offers its own depth — criteria of evidence and ethical limits, program design and evaluation, engineering and statistical modeling — and all converge on a shared axis: public education.
